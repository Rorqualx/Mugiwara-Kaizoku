# External Algorithms and Best Practices for Multi-Source Manga Metadata Aggregation

Research date: 2026-10-10. Scope: external literature, official docs and open-source projects only (no codebase inspection). Verification note: items under "Cited Findings" were read from the linked page or PDF during this session unless marked "(search snippet only)". Items under "Inferences" are my synthesis; where an inference leans on general background knowledge that I did not re-verify against a primary source in this session, it is marked "(background, unverified here)".

## Key Question 1: Entity resolution / record linkage for titles

### Takeaway
The classic comparative evidence (Cohen, Ravikumar and Fienberg 2003) favours hybrid token+character measures (SoftTFIDF with Jaro-Winkler inner similarity, theta = 0.9) over any single edit-distance or token measure, and the record-linkage tradition (Fellegi-Sunter, as implemented in Splink) favours summing independent per-feature evidence weights into a match probability rather than thresholding one fuzzy score. For a small catalogue the practical design is: cheap high-recall blocking (trigram or token index), a multi-feature scorer (title variants, year, format, author, volume count), a "margin between best and second-best" ambiguity rule, and thresholds calibrated on a labeled set.

### Cited Findings
- Cohen et al. compared edit-distance metrics, "fast heuristic string comparators", token-based metrics and hybrids; "the best-performing method is a hybrid scheme combining a TFIDF weighting scheme ... with the Jaro-Winkler string-distance scheme" — [Cohen, Ravikumar, Fienberg 2003, IJCAI IIWeb workshop (PDF)](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~wcohen/postscript/ijcai-ws-2003.pdf). Flag: 2003 paper, English name/record datasets, not multilingual titles.
- Among token-based metrics, "TFIDF seems generally the best"; among edit-distance-like methods Monge-Elkan "does best on average, but the Jaro and Jaro-Winkler methods are close in average performance, and do noticably better than Monge-Elkan on several problems", and the Jaro variants took "about 1/10 the time of the Monge-Elkan method" — [Cohen et al. 2003](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~wcohen/postscript/ijcai-ws-2003.pdf).
- "SoftTFIDF is generally the best among the hybrid methods" and "the best overall distance measure for these datasets"; the experiments "used Jaro-Winkler as a secondary distance and theta = 0.9" (tokens count as matching when their Jaro-Winkler similarity exceeds 0.9, then TF-IDF weighted) — [Cohen et al. 2003](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~wcohen/postscript/ijcai-ws-2003.pdf).
- The authors caution that the suite "is dominated by problems from one source" and "a different distribution of problems might well lead to quite different results"; token-based methods "perform poorly on the census-like dataset, which contains many misspellings" — [Cohen et al. 2003](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~wcohen/postscript/ijcai-ws-2003.pdf).
- The paper notes Jaro and Jaro-Winkler "seem to be intended primarily for short strings (e.g., personal first or last names)" — [Cohen et al. 2003](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~wcohen/postscript/ijcai-ws-2003.pdf).
- Blocking is cheap and near-lossless at this scale: a token blocker found "between 93.3% and 100.0% of the correct pairs, with an average of 98.9%", and a 4-gram blocker averaged 99.0% — [Cohen et al. 2003](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~wcohen/postscript/ijcai-ws-2003.pdf).
- Evaluation method used in that study: rank candidate pairs by score, report non-interpolated average precision and maximum F1 per dataset (i.e. threshold-free comparison first, threshold choice second) — [Cohen et al. 2003](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~wcohen/postscript/ijcai-ws-2003.pdf).
- Fellegi-Sunter as documented by Splink: for each comparison feature, m = Pr(observation | match) and u = Pr(observation | non-match); the partial match weight is log2(m/u); the total weight is log2(lambda/(1-lambda)) + sum of partial weights, converted to probability 2^M/(1+2^M); a weight of 0 is probability 0.5 and a weight of 7 is about 0.99. It assumes comparisons are conditionally independent — [Splink: The Fellegi-Sunter model](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/topic_guides/theory/fellegi_sunter.html).
- Parameter estimation in Splink: u is estimated directly from random record pairs (random pairs are almost all non-matches); the prior lambda is estimated from deterministic high-precision rules plus a guessed recall, and being within roughly half/double the true value is usually adequate; m is estimated by EM on blocked pairs, repeated with different blocking keys because a blocked column cannot get an m estimate; when EM misbehaves m values can be fixed manually — [Splink: training rationale](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/topic_guides/training/training_rationale.html).
- PostgreSQL pg_trgm: `similarity()` is whole-string trigram overlap; `word_similarity()` is the greatest similarity between the first string and any continuous extent of the second (good for a short query inside a longer title); `strict_word_similarity()` forces extent boundaries onto word boundaries. Default thresholds are 0.3, 0.6 and 0.5 respectively. Non-alphanumeric characters are ignored and comparison is case-insensitive. Both GIN and GiST indexes support the threshold operators, but only GiST supports `ORDER BY col <-> 'q' LIMIT k` nearest-neighbour ordering — [PostgreSQL docs: pg_trgm](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/pgtrgm.html).
- MusicBrainz Picard exposes, besides minimum-similarity thresholds, a "minimum margin between best and second-best match" option: if the gap is below the margin the match is flagged ambiguous for manual verification. Its docs warn that lowering similarity percentages finds more matches "but may also produce false positives" — [Picard docs: Matching options](https://picard-docs.musicbrainz.org/en/latest/config/options_matching.html).
- Kavita+ auto-accepts a match only when there is exactly one 100% match or exactly one match scored 90% or higher; otherwise the series goes to a failed-match queue for admin resolution, and users can force a match with a URL or short code such as `anilist:123` — [Kavita wiki: Kavita+ metadata](https://wiki.kavitareader.com/kavita+/metadata/); [Kavita+ FAQ (search snippet only)](https://wiki.kavitareader.com/kavita+/faq/).
- Embeddings: a 2022 study aligning cross-lingual Wikidata labels across 10 languages reported that sentence-embedding methods outperformed the alternatives, including across scripts — [arXiv:2206.08709 (search snippet only, not read in full)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08709v1).

### Inferences
- Recommended title scorer, sized for TypeScript/Postgres: (1) normalise (NFKC, case-fold, strip punctuation, normalise long vowels and common romanisation variants such as ou/o/ō, wo/o, remove or separately capture edition/sequel markers like "Part 2", "Season 2", "II", ":re", subtitle after colon or dash); (2) compare the local title against every alternate title the provider exposes (romaji, English, native, synonyms) and take the max; (3) compute at least two complementary scores, one character-level (Jaro-Winkler or trigram) and one token-level (token-set ratio or SoftTFIDF-style), because Cohen et al. show each family fails on different data; (4) treat sequel/number tokens as a separate exact-match feature with strong negative weight on disagreement, since "Title" vs "Title 2" is a near-identical string but a different entity.
- A Fellegi-Sunter-style additive score is a natural drop-in replacement for a single threshold: features such as title-similarity band (exact / high / medium / low), start-year agreement (exact / plus-minus 1 / differ / missing), format agreement (manga vs light novel vs one-shot), author overlap, and volume-count closeness each contribute log2(m/u). With a few hundred labeled pairs, m and u can simply be counted from the labels instead of running EM; u can also be estimated from random title-candidate pairs exactly as Splink does. The independence assumption is violated (title variants correlate), so treat the output as a ranking score and calibrate the accept threshold empirically.
- Threshold calibration procedure: build a labeled set of (local title, candidate, is-match) triples including hard negatives (spin-offs, light-novel originals, anthologies, same-name works); sweep the threshold, plot precision/recall, and choose two cut points, an auto-accept point at a target precision (for example 99%) and an auto-reject point, with the band in between sent to review. Add a Picard-style margin rule (best minus runner-up below margin means ambiguous) because most wrong bindings in franchise-heavy catalogues come from a close runner-up, not a low absolute score.
- Blocking at this scale is about API cost, not CPU: the "block" is whatever each provider's search endpoint returns. Issue two or three query variants (romaji, English, native) per provider and union the candidates; locally, pg_trgm GIN indexes on a normalised alias table are sufficient for dedup of thousands of titles.
- Embedding similarity is best treated as a candidate-generation or tie-break feature for cross-script cases (native Japanese vs English licensed title), not as the decision rule; ID bridging (Key Question 2) solves most cross-script cases more reliably and more cheaply.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed benchmark specific to manga/anime title matching or to romanisation variants was found; the Cohen et al. evidence is from 2003 English datasets and its ranking of measures may not transfer to short multilingual titles.
- Christen's record-linkage book and more recent learned-matcher literature (for example deep entity matching) were not retrieved in this session, so no claims about them are made here.
- The embedding claim rests on a search snippet; I found no 2024-2026 paper directly comparing multilingual embeddings against fuzzy string measures for short media titles.
- Token-set / token-sort ratio (RapidFuzz/FuzzyWuzzy family) has no formal evaluation that I located; its usefulness for subtitle and word-order variance is practitioner lore.

## Key Question 2: ID bridging and cross-reference datasets

### Takeaway
For manga (as opposed to anime) the usable bridges are MangaDex's `links` map, AniList's `idMal`, Wikidata external-ID properties, and MangaBaka's merged database/API; the well-known community mapping projects (anime-offline-database, Fribb/anime-lists, arm) are anime-only. Every bridge is crowd- or heuristically-maintained, so a bridged ID should be treated as a very strong prior to be verified with a cheap plausibility check, not as ground truth.

### Cited Findings
- MangaDex manga `links` keys and formats: `al` AniList ID, `ap` Anime-Planet slug, `bw` BookWalker slug, `mu` MangaUpdates ID, `nu` NovelUpdates slug, `kt` Kitsu (integer ID or slug), `mal` MyAnimeList ID, plus `amz`, `ebj`, `cdj`, `raw`, `engtl` as full URLs — [MangaDex API docs: Static data](https://api.mangadex.org/docs/3-enumerations/).
- MangaDex status enumeration is `ongoing`, `completed`, `hiatus`, `cancelled`; related-manga types include `main_story`, `side_story`, `spin_off`, `prequel`, `sequel`, `adapted_from`, `alternate_version`, `colored`, `doujinshi` and others (useful for detecting spin-off/variant bindings) — [MangaDex API docs: Static data](https://api.mangadex.org/docs/3-enumerations/).
- Wikidata has external-ID properties P4087 "MyAnimeList manga ID" (formatter `https://myanimelist.net/manga/$1`), P8731 "AniList manga ID", P11149 "MangaUpdates manga ID" (formatter `https://www.mangaupdates.com/series/$1`), P11348 "Anime-Planet manga ID", and P1984 "Anime News Network manga ID" — [Wikidata P4087](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P4087); [Wikidata P11149](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P11149).
- AniList's own guidance notes that MyAnimeList can take a long time to approve works, "more so for manga", so MAL links on AniList entries can be missing for a while — [AniList: MAL ID guidance (search snippet only)](https://anilist.notion.site/MAL-ID-d66c5daf55c744e39f57c955f6048f3d).
- MangaBaka exposes an API at `https://api.mangabaka.org/` cross-referencing AniList, Anime-Planet, Kitsu, MangaUpdates, MyAnimeList and Shikimori; full database refreshed weekly, individual series every 1 to 7 days; rate limits 30 req/min for search and 180 req/min for other GETs (per IP, leaky bucket, only uncached requests count); downloadable database dumps are offered; MangaBaka-original data is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and third-party data remains under each provider's terms; no 1.0 schema stability guarantee — [MangaBaka API page](https://mangabaka.org/api).
- Both Komf and Kavita+ now use MangaBaka as a provider: Komf lists `mangaBaka` with `mode: API or DATABASE`, and Kavita+ lists MangaBaka as its manga source — [Komf README](https://github.com/Snd-R/komf); [Kavita wiki: Kavita+ metadata](https://wiki.kavitareader.com/kavita+/metadata/).
- manami-project/anime-offline-database covers anime only (10 providers, 41,537 entries, 65% "reviewed" as of week 27 2026), is licensed ODbL 1.0 + DbCL 1.0, published weekly JSON/JSONL releases, and the repository was archived (read-only) on 2026-07-04 — [anime-offline-database README](https://github.com/manami-project/anime-offline-database).
- Fribb/anime-lists merges anime-offline-database with Anime-Lists on the AniDB ID and covers AniDB, AniList, Anime-Planet, Kitsu, MAL, TVDB, TMDB and others; it does not accept corrections (errors must be fixed upstream) and states no license — [Fribb/anime-lists README](https://github.com/Fribb/anime-lists).
- BeeeQueue/arm-server maps anime IDs between AniList, AniDB, MAL and Kitsu and is built on Fribb/anime-lists refreshed every 24 hours — [arm-server listing (search snippet only)](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2FBeeeQueue%2Farm-server).

### Inferences
- Suggested bridge order for a manga pipeline: (1) user/manual binding; (2) provider-native cross-links fetched anyway (AniList `idMal`; MangaDex `links.al/mal/mu/kt`); (3) MangaBaka lookup by any known ID to fill the remaining providers in one call; (4) Wikidata SPARQL on P8731/P4087/P11149 for Wikipedia/Wikidata QIDs, which also bridges to Wikipedia article titles; (5) title search as the last resort. Record which path produced each binding so that a wrong bridge can be traced and durably rejected.
- Bridges carry correlated error: MangaDex links are uploader/moderator-entered and MangaBaka's merge is itself an entity-resolution output, so two "agreeing" bridges may share one upstream mistake. A cheap verification gate (title similarity above a low floor, format equals manga, start year within one, volume count not wildly different) on every bridged ID catches the typical failure mode of a manga entry linked to its light-novel or spin-off sibling.
- Because anime-offline-database is now archived, anything depending on it or on downstream merges (Fribb, arm) should be considered at risk of going stale; this matters only if anime IDs are used as a stepping stone.
- MangaBaka's NC-SA license and unstable schema argue for using it as a lookup service with local caching rather than redistributing its dump.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm Wikidata property numbers for a MangaDex title ID or a Kitsu manga ID; neither appears in the "related properties" of P4087, and searches did not surface property pages. Their existence is unverified.
- Completeness figures (what fraction of AniList/MAL manga have a Wikidata item, or what fraction of MangaDex titles carry `al`/`mal`/`mu` links) were not found in any source; these would have to be measured empirically against the local library.
- Kitsu's `mappings` resource (externalSite values such as `myanimelist/manga`) could not be confirmed from official Kitsu docs; only third-party wrappers describing anime mappings were found.
- AniList docs for `idMal` and Wikidata's own license terms (CC0) were not fetched this session.
- No evidence was found of a maintained, openly licensed, manga-specific mapping list comparable to anime-offline-database other than MangaBaka.

## Key Question 3: Truth discovery / data fusion for conflicting field values

### Takeaway
The literature's consistent findings are that (a) reliability-weighted fusion beats naive voting only when the reliability estimates are accurate, (b) no method is consistently best, (c) reliability should be estimated per attribute, and (d) undetected copying among sources is the main way weighted fusion goes wrong. For 6-8 sources and thousands of titles, supervised per-source per-field accuracy estimated from a gold set (with smoothing), a hand-declared dependency grouping for known lineage, and robust estimators for numeric counts are more defensible than unsupervised iterative algorithms.

### Cited Findings
- Truth discovery's premise: voting/averaging "assume all the sources are equally reliable"; truth discovery instead estimates source reliability jointly with truths. The general procedure initialises source weights and then iterates truth computation and weight estimation until the change is below a threshold — [Li et al., "A Survey on Truth Discovery", arXiv:1505.02463](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.02463). Flag: 2015 survey; still the standard reference but predates LLM-era work.
- The survey catalogues TruthFinder, AccuSim, AccuCopy ("improves AccuSim, and considers the copying relations among sources"), CRH (a framework for heterogeneous data types), SSTF (semi-supervised, "a small set of labeled truths are incorporated to guide the source reliability estimation") and others, and notes that for categorical data the distance function can be 0-1 loss — [Li et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.02463).
- Copy detection principle: copiers "make the same mistakes with sources they copy from"; sharing false values is far stronger evidence of dependence than sharing true values — [Li et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.02463).
- Sources with few claims: the survey highlights confidence-interval approaches (CATD, "A confidence-aware approach for truth discovery on long-tail data", PVLDB 2015) that use the confidence interval of the reliability estimate rather than a point estimate for "small" sources — [Li et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.02463).
- Initialisation matters: with uniform initial weights "the behavior of truth discovery is identical to voting/averaging" at the start, which fails in settings where "the majority of sources provide inaccurate information"; "even a small set of labeled truths could improve the performance", and most methods "can be modified to take advantage of labeled truths" — [Li et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.02463).
- Evaluation metrics in the field: accuracy/error rate for categorical data and Mean Absolute Error for continuous data; the survey also lists fine-grained (per-topic) source reliability as an established extension — [Li et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.02463).
- Empirical baseline on real web data (Stock and Flight domains): "for 70% data items more than one value is provided"; of the inconsistency, nearly 50% is due to ambiguity (semantic or instance), 20% to out-of-date data and 30% to pure mistakes; only 70% of correct values are provided by a majority of sources — [Li et al., "Truth Finding on the Deep Web: Is the Problem Solved?", PVLDB 2012, arXiv:1503.00303](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00303). Flag: 2012 data; domains differ from manga metadata.
- Dominance predicts correctness: in Stock, where the dominant value was backed by over half the sources, 98% of dominant values matched the gold standard; at dominance factor 0.4 this fell to 84%, and at 0.1 the top value was right only 43% of the time — [Li et al. 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00303).
- Fusion methods found correct values for 96% of items on average, but "we did not find one method that is consistently better than others"; considering trustworthiness, copying, value similarity and formatting helps, yet "it is essential that accurate information on source trustworthiness and copying between sources is used; otherwise, fusion accuracy can even be harmed" — [Li et al. 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00303).
- Copying matters in both directions: on Flight, AccuCopy improved over VOTE by 9% (about half of VOTE's mistakes) because copying was mainly among low-accuracy sources; on Stock, VOTE improved from .908 to .923 simply by excluding known copiers. AccuCopy did poorly on numeric Stock data because its copy detector ignored value similarity and treated near-true values as shared falsehoods — [Li et al. 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00303).
- More sources is not monotonically better: fusing a few high-recall sources (5 for Stock, 9 for Flight) gave the highest recall and "adding more sources afterwards can only hurt"; naive voting often had lower recall than the single best source — [Li et al. 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00303).
- "Distinguishing trustworthiness for different attributes" improved precision, while item-level trustworthiness (3-Estimates) did not help much; all methods beat VOTE when given sampled (i.e. gold-derived) trustworthiness, "showing that the problem lies in trustworthiness computation" — [Li et al. 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00303).
- Numeric values need tolerance bucketing before voting: the study grouped numeric claims into buckets around the dominant value with a tolerance factor alpha = 0.01 before counting agreement — [Li et al. 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00303).
- About 50% of residual fusion errors were fixable with correct trust and copying knowledge, 10% were granularity differences rather than errors, and for 40% the data simply did not support the truth — [Li et al. 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00303).
- Domain lineage evidence: Jikan is a scraper of MyAnimeList ("scrapes the website to satisfy the need for API functionality that MyAnimeList.net lacks"), so Jikan and MAL are one source, not two — [jikan-rest README](https://github.com/jikan-me/jikan-rest).

### Inferences
- Practical fusion recipe for this scale. Categorical fields (status, format): per-field accuracy-weighted vote where each source's vote weight is the log-odds of its measured accuracy on that field (the ACCU-style vote weight; background, unverified here), with accuracies taken from a gold set and Laplace/Beta smoothing so a source with 5 observations cannot reach weight infinity; the CATD idea of using the lower confidence bound of accuracy for sparsely observed sources is the principled version of this. Numeric counts (volumes, chapters): bucket near-equal values, then take a weighted median rather than a weighted mean so that one catastrophic source cannot drag the result (weighted median as the minimiser of weighted absolute loss, as in CRH for continuous data; background, unverified here).
- Counts on ongoing series are not a pure "conflicting claims" problem: they are monotone non-decreasing over time, and the deep-web study attributes 20-34% of disagreement to out-of-date data. That argues for freshness-aware rules: for ongoing series prefer the maximum among sources that pass a plausibility check and are trusted for that field (stale sources under-report), while for completed series use accuracy-weighted vote/median. Semantic ambiguity (the largest cause of inconsistency in the study, near 50%) maps directly to manga problems: chapters counted with or without extras/half chapters, volumes in tankobon vs bunko vs omnibus vs English edition, "status" meaning original publication vs scanlation vs English release. Normalising semantics per source before fusion will remove more error than a smarter voting rule.
- Dependence handling without statistics: declare source groups by known lineage (Jikan is MAL; MangaBaka redistributes AniList/MAL/MU/Kitsu values; MangaDex/Kitsu entries are often seeded from MAL/AniList by users; Wikipedia and Fandom editors cite each other) and cap each group at one vote, or discount within-group agreement. A data-driven check is cheap at this scale: for each source pair, measure agreement on values that the gold set says are wrong; high shared-error rate indicates copying, following the AccuCopy principle, but use tolerance for numerics to avoid the false positives the 2012 study observed.
- Unsupervised iterative methods (TruthFinder, Dawid-Skene EM, CRH) are computationally trivial here but risky: with 6-8 sources, partially dependent, uniform initialisation starts from majority vote and can lock onto a copying clique. If used, initialise from gold-derived accuracies and treat them as a monitoring signal (does the unsupervised estimate of a source's reliability drift away from the gold-derived one?) rather than as the production resolver.
- Keeping reliabilities current: recompute per-source per-field accuracy from the gold set on every harness run; additionally log "source disagreed with final fused value" per source/field as a cheap continuous proxy, and trigger gold-set review when that rate shifts. User manual overrides are free labels: each one is a (field, true value) observation that can be appended to the calibration set.
- "Fewer, better sources per field" is supported by the evidence: it is legitimate for a field's resolver to ignore sources whose measured accuracy on that field is below the best single source, instead of including all 8 with small weights.
- Emit a confidence alongside each fused value (for example dominance factor of the winning bucket weighted by source accuracy); the 2012 study shows dominance is strongly predictive of correctness, so low-dominance fields are the right ones to queue for re-fetch or review.

### Gaps
- Dong and Srivastava's data-fusion tutorial/book, the original ACCU/POPACCU papers, Dawid-Skene, and the CRH and CATD papers were not read directly; they are represented here only through the two Li et al. papers. Exact formulas (ACCU vote weight, CRH weight update) are therefore given as background, not as verified citations.
- No study was found that evaluates truth-discovery methods on anime/manga metadata or measures actual copying between Kitsu, MAL and AniList; the lineage statements for those three are domain assumptions, not cited facts (only the Jikan-MAL relationship is documented).
- No literature was located on fusing monotone, time-evolving counts specifically (the survey mentions dynamic/streaming truth discovery but details were not extracted).

## Key Question 4: How comparable open-source projects solve this

### Takeaway
None of the comparable projects reviewed performs statistical fusion: they use ordered provider priority with first-match-wins and optional fill-missing aggregation, conservative auto-accept thresholds with a manual-match escape hatch, user field locks that outrank all automation, and embedded provider IDs in folder names or sidecar files to bypass matching altogether. A weighted-voting pipeline is already more ambitious than the norm; what the peers do better is the override/lock/manual-bind model and explicit failure queues.

### Cited Findings
- Komf (Komga/Kavita metadata fetcher): 14 providers each with a numeric `priority` (lower first) and `enabled` flag (only MangaUpdates on by default; AniList, MAL, MangaDex, ComicVine, MangaBaka, Nautiljon, Bangumi, publishers etc. available). Default behaviour takes metadata from the "first positive match in configured providers by order of priority". With `aggregate: true`, remaining providers are also searched and a field is filled from a lower-priority provider only if earlier providers had no data for it; `mergeTags`/`mergeGenres` union those list fields across providers. Per-field toggles (`seriesMetadata`, `bookMetadata`), per-library provider sets and per-library update settings exist; `mediaType` filters candidates during matching; ComicVine is labelled experimental and an ID can be forced via a folder-name pattern such as `[cv-{id}]` — [Komf README](https://github.com/Snd-R/komf).
- Kavita+: sources are MangaBaka (manga/webtoons), Hardcover (books/light novels) and ComicBookRoundup (comics); default budget of 50 metadata matches/refreshes per day (ad-hoc UI requests exempt); auto-accept only on exactly one 100% match or exactly one match at 90% or above; failures go to a Manage Metadata queue; a Match modal lets users fix, re-pull, or set "Do Not Match"; edition choice (for example omnibus vs paperback) changes volume counts and therefore publication status — [Kavita wiki: Kavita+ metadata](https://wiki.kavitareader.com/kavita+/metadata/).
- Kavita will not overwrite a field if the setting is off or "the underlying field is locked AND there is no override setup" — [Kavita wiki (search snippet only)](https://wiki.kavitareader.com/kavita+/metadata/).
- Komga does no online fetching in core: metadata comes from ComicInfo.xml, EPUB metadata, Mylar `series.json`, local artwork and ISBN barcodes. Series-level values are aggregated from books by simple rules: most frequent value for title/publisher/language/reading direction, highest value for age rating, union for genres; total book count is the highest ComicInfo `Count` or Mylar `total_issues`; status comes from Mylar's `status` — [Komga docs: scan, analysis, refresh](https://komga.org/docs/guides/scan-analysis-refresh/).
- Mylar3 uses ComicVine as its sole metadata source for series and issues and for subsequent updates; its `series.json` sidecar schema carries the ComicVine series ID, year, volume and `total_issues` — [Mylar3 wiki: Where Mylar gets the metadata from (search snippet only)](https://github.com/mylar3/mylar3/wiki/Where-Mylar-gets-the-metadata-from); [Mylar3 wiki: series.json schema (search snippet only)](https://github.com/mylar3/mylar3/wiki/series.json-schema-%28version-1.0.1%29).
- MusicBrainz Picard: configurable minimum similarity for cluster-to-release lookups and for file-to-track matching, a best-vs-second-best margin that flags ambiguous matches, a duration tolerance (default 2 seconds), and unmatched files are parked in an explicit "unmatched files" group rather than force-assigned — [Picard docs: Matching options](https://picard-docs.musicbrainz.org/en/latest/config/options_matching.html).
- Jellyfin: metadata comes from multiple providers (TMDb, OMDb, local .nfo by default; TheTVDB, AniDB, fanart.tv via plugins) — [Jellyfin docs: Metadata](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/server/metadata/).
- calibre: downloads from Google Books and Amazon; matching uses ISBN in preference to title/author when an ISBN is present — [calibre manual: Editing e-book metadata](https://manual.calibre-ebook.com/metadata.html).

### Inferences
- Common pattern across peers (priority waterfall plus fill-missing) is equivalent to a degenerate weighted vote where the top provider has infinite weight per field. Its advantage is explainability; its weakness is that it never detects that the top provider is wrong. A hybrid worth auditing against: keep weighted fusion, but expose a per-field provider ordering as the tie-break and make every fused value explainable in the UI ("volumes = 34 from AniList + MangaUpdates; Kitsu said 33").
- Precedence model worth copying, highest first: user-locked field value; user manual binding of an entity; sidecar/embedded IDs (ComicInfo, `series.json`, `[provider-id]` in folder name); bridged IDs; fuzzy-matched IDs. Automation must never overwrite a locked field and must never silently re-bind a manually bound entity.
- Kavita's "exactly one candidate at or above threshold" rule and Picard's margin rule are both uniqueness tests; implementing one is cheap and directly targets spin-off/sequel confusion.
- Komga's series-from-books aggregation rules (mode for scalar, max for age rating and count, union for lists) are a reasonable default for the local-files layer before any online source is consulted.
- Edition awareness (Kavita) is the practical answer to volume-count disagreement: store which edition a volume count refers to instead of forcing one number.

### Gaps
- Sonarr/Radarr, Plex agents and Suwayomi/Tachiyomi tracker matching were not researched in this session; no claims are made about them. (Background, unverified here: Sonarr/Radarr rely on a single canonical metadata source per media type with ID-based lookup, and Tachiyomi-family trackers bind via user-confirmed search results rather than automatic matching.)

## Key Question 5: Pipeline engineering (rate limits, caching, scheduling, provenance, evaluation)

### Takeaway
Each provider publishes different and sometimes shifting limits (AniList is currently degraded to 30 req/min), so the client needs per-provider token buckets driven by response headers, honouring `Retry-After`, with an adaptive concurrency cap on top; caching should follow stale-while-revalidate and stale-if-error semantics; and evaluation should be a per-field gold-set harness reporting accuracy (categorical) and absolute error (numeric) alongside match precision/recall.

### Cited Findings
- AniList: documented limit 90 requests/minute, but a warning states the API "is currently in a degraded state and is limited to 30 requests per minute". Responses carry `X-RateLimit-Limit` and `X-RateLimit-Remaining`; exceeding the limit gives a 1-minute timeout with HTTP 429 plus `Retry-After` (seconds) and `X-RateLimit-Reset` (Unix timestamp); a separate burst limiter exists; rate-limit raise requests are not currently accepted — [AniList API docs: Rate limiting](https://docs.anilist.co/guide/rate-limiting).
- ComicVine: the API "officially supports 200 requests per resource, per hour" per user, and velocity detection can temporarily block clients making too many requests per second — [ComicVine API page (search snippet only)](https://comicvine.gamespot.com/api). A third-party library (comicbox) uses 1 req/sec and 200 req/hr as its ComicVine defaults — [comicbox rate limits (search snippet only)](https://comicbox.readthedocs.io/src/comicbox/formats/base/online/rate_limits/).
- MangaUpdates acceptable-use policy: credit MangaUpdates, use "reasonable spacing between requests", and "employ caching mechanisms when accessing data"; no numeric limit is published — [MangaUpdates API docs](https://api.mangaupdates.com/).
- MangaBaka: 30 req/min search, 180 req/min other GETs, per-IP leaky bucket, only uncached requests count, 429 on excess; `cf-cache-status: HIT` shows cache hits — [MangaBaka API page](https://mangabaka.org/api).
- Jikan is an unofficial MyAnimeList scraper; its own tests can fail with HTTP 429 from MyAnimeList, and it can be self-hosted via Docker — [jikan-rest README](https://github.com/jikan-me/jikan-rest).
- RFC 5861 defines `stale-while-revalidate=N` (a cache may serve a stale response for up to N seconds while revalidating in the background without blocking the request) and `stale-if-error=N` (a stale response may be served when the origin would return 500/502/503/504, up to N seconds past freshness). Example: `Cache-Control: max-age=600, stale-if-error=1200` — [RFC 5861](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5861).
- Netflix's concurrency-limits library treats a concurrency limit like a TCP congestion window and discovers it automatically; Vegas estimates queue as `L * (1 - minRTT/sampleRTT)` and nudges the limit up or down by 1 against alpha/beta bounds; Gradient2 compares short- and long-term latency averages; AIMD is a pure loss-based limiter; timeouts and rejections trigger aggressive back-off — [Netflix/concurrency-limits README](https://github.com/Netflix/concurrency-limits).
- Refresh cadence used by a comparable aggregator: MangaBaka refreshes individual series "every 1 to 7 days, depending on how often the source sites update them" and the full database weekly; Kavita+ budgets 50 automatic matches/refreshes per day by default — [MangaBaka API page](https://mangabaka.org/api); [Kavita wiki: Kavita+ metadata](https://wiki.kavitareader.com/kavita+/metadata/).
- Evaluation conventions: matching is evaluated by ranking candidate pairs and reporting average precision and max F1 — [Cohen et al. 2003](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~wcohen/postscript/ijcai-ws-2003.pdf); fusion is evaluated by accuracy/error rate for categorical fields and MAE for continuous fields — [Li et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.02463); the deep-web study additionally reports precision of the dominant value by dominance factor and classifies residual errors by cause (trust estimate, copying, granularity, unsupported by data) — [Li et al. 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00303).

### Inferences
- Rate limiting: one limiter per provider (not global), configured from published numbers but corrected live from headers: when `X-RateLimit-Remaining` is present, pace to it; on 429, pause the whole provider queue for `Retry-After` (not just the failing request) and add jitter on resume. Because AniList's effective limit has changed from 90 to 30 without an API version change, limits must be data-driven rather than constants. For providers that publish no numbers (MangaUpdates, Fandom/Wikipedia), an AIMD concurrency cap (increase by 1 per clean window, halve on 429/5xx/timeout) is a small, robust substitute for a hand-tuned delay.
- Request coalescing: de-duplicate identical in-flight requests by a canonical key (provider + endpoint + normalised params) so that concurrent enrichments of related titles share one call; batch where the API allows (AniList GraphQL can fetch multiple IDs per query via aliases or `id_in`; background, unverified here).
- Caching tiers: positive cache with per-status TTL; application-level stale-while-revalidate (serve cached, enqueue refresh) and stale-if-error (keep serving last good payload during provider outages, and surface the outage) mirroring RFC 5861; negative cache for "search returned no candidate" with a shorter TTL and exponential growth on repeated misses, kept distinct from "request failed", which must not be cached as a negative. Use ETag/`If-None-Match` only where a provider is observed to emit validators; I could not confirm validator support for any of the listed providers.
- Re-enrichment scheduling: tier by volatility. Ongoing/releasing series: refresh on the order of days (MangaBaka's 1-7 day range is a reasonable prior); hiatus: weeks; completed/cancelled: months, or only on demand. Refine with an adaptive rule: shorten the interval when the last refresh changed a tracked field, lengthen it (up to a cap) when nothing changed. Spend the budget by priority (user-visible, recently read, low-confidence fields first), in the spirit of Kavita's daily budget.
- Provenance storage (Postgres): keep an append-only observations table, one row per (title, provider, field, raw value, normalised value, fetched_at, provider entity id, request fingerprint), and a resolved table per (title, field) holding the chosen value, confidence, resolver version, the list of supporting and dissenting observation ids, and a lock/override flag with actor and timestamp. This makes fusion a pure, re-runnable function of observations, enables replaying a new resolver over historical data without refetching, and supports the copy-detection and reliability statistics in Key Question 3.
- Evaluation harness: (1) a gold set stratified by difficulty (franchise with spin-offs, one-shots, manhwa/manhua, long-running ongoing, completed classics, obscure titles), with truth taken from publisher or primary sources and stamped with an as-of date, since counts for ongoing series expire; (2) matching metrics per provider: precision, recall, wrong-target rate (more harmful than a miss) and abstention rate; (3) fusion metrics per field: exact-match accuracy for categorical, MAE and exact-match rate for counts, plus the same metrics for each single source and for naive majority vote as baselines, because the literature shows fusion can underperform the best single source; (4) error taxonomy per miss (wrong binding, semantic mismatch such as edition, stale source, source error, unsupported by any source); (5) frozen recorded provider responses so the harness is deterministic and can gate changes as a regression test, with a separate periodic live run to detect provider drift.
